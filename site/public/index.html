A neurosymbolic software factory for high-assurance FCIS applications

AI proposes. Exact checkers decide.

ZenoFCIS builds a working application from a reviewed contract. The library makes every decision from that contract and checks its laws before anything is stored. Models, agents and synthesis can propose programs and repairs; none of them can approve its own work.

How the factory works

People, models, synthesis and checkers each do one job

Functional core, imperative shell: the core decides and the shell acts. ZenoFCIS makes that boundary enforceable, then puts a checker at every point where a proposal enters.

  1. Specify

    The contract states the state, the commands, the decisions and the laws they must keep. People review it, helped by gap finding, focused questions and teach-back.

  2. Propose

    Models, agents and synthesis propose programs, repairs, sketches and smaller equivalent programs. A proposal is only a proposal.

  3. Decide

    Exact checkers accept or refuse each proposal. A refusal comes back with the failing input, so the next proposal can fix it.

  4. Build and run

    zeno-fcis new --contract generates the application. Its SQLite shell stores each decision with a receipt and delivers effects through an outbox.

Evidence

Every claim says how it was checked

ZenoFCIS states each guarantee at its true strength, over a named domain, with its trusted assumptions.

Tested

Examples and properties run against the code.

The account-lockout demo replays 20 independent decision examples in your browser.

Checked on every input

Exhaustive over a declared finite domain. Nothing in that domain is skipped.

Inventory reservation: 864 inputs. Agent treasury guard: 6,144 fact tuples. A withdrawal-queue upgrade compares 1,296,000 input tuples.

Attested

An SMT solver's result, recorded with its inputs. The solver itself is trusted.

CVC5 attests that the withdrawal queue's action laws alone keep the vault solvent, over the declared integer ranges.

Kernel-checked

A proof checked by the Lean 4 kernel.

The 2.5.0 source includes an exact-value theorem for the ZenoDEX Spot pool's algebraic contract; linking it to executable code remains open.

Proved

Verus proofs that library code meets its mathematical contract.

Admission, original-byte interpretation, scalar execution, complete decisions, laws and genesis, authority and replay.

Releases

Current release source and plans

2.5.0 is the current release source. Final qualification and publication remain separate release gates. Plans change; a planned item is a direction, not a promise.

2.4.0RELEASEDOctober 2026
  • Money math in checked rules. Exact multiply-then-divide with declared floor or ceiling, a full-width intermediate, and a refusal when the result does not fit. Values never wrap or saturate. Two- and three-factor product comparisons cover fees, swap outputs, liquidity mint and burn, and quorum.
  • AI proposes, the checker decides. Repair from exact failing inputs, sketch completion, families and transfer, program search for smaller equivalent programs, capture of a Python function as a checked core, and learning from examples.
  • Help with the specification. A gap finder, focused questions, teach-back, invariant checks with counterexamples, scenarios and probes, state profiles, and decomposition with blame.
  • Private knowledge. A Datalog reasoning engine over your own knowledge bases, with provenance. Your knowledge stays out of source and release packages.
  • Two platforms. Linux x86_64 and Apple silicon macOS, each with its own build and installed-binary checks.

Limits in 2.4.0: amounts in rules are 64-bit; contained AI loops need Linux with a systemd user session; the component catalog's laws do not fully determine its decisions.

2.5.0RELEASE SOURCE
  • Wider, safer arithmetic. 128-bit amounts and signed floor division, with existing 64-bit programs keeping their meaning. Declared units and scales such as e8 prices and basis points, with unit mistakes refused. Chains of up to 16 rounded steps, each matched against a big-integer reference.
  • Loops on more machines. The loops work on money math. Their finite checks run contained without systemd, so they work on macOS, in CI and in containers. Capturing a Python function still needs Linux.
  • ZenoDEX Spot. A constant-product exchange core on ZenoFCIS. "No free value" is checked over 9.1 billion single-user operation sequences, and a Lean-checked theorem shows a group of accounts cannot gain exact value over any number of operations. Linking that theorem to the executable code is still open.
  • Sturdier evidence. Knowledge evidence runs on a private snapshot of the bytes it checked, and qualification runs its controls in parallel.
2.5.1PLANNED
  • ZenoDEX stablecoin vault and oracle. A zUSD vault and a price oracle with three independent reporters; stale or replayed reports are refused.
  • Governance as checked rules. Signer thresholds, timelocks from announcement, outflow caps, and an exit that always stays open.
  • Search for loss paths before release. The loops search for operation sequences, including chains across components, that could lose user funds. Each one found becomes a law the system must keep.
  • Rules over time. Rules about sequences of events, and knowledge that spans several domains.
LaterPLANNED
  • More of each application under checked rules, including its user-interface logic.
  • Wider domains and more platforms, with every guarantee still stated at its true strength.

Scope

What the checks do not claim

  • That the rules say what you meant

    The checks prove the code follows the contract. People still review whether the contract captures the intended behaviour.

  • That outside facts are true

    A deployment must authenticate callers and supply trusted time and prices. The core takes them as inputs.

  • That the shell is proved

    Storage and delivery are tested, not formally verified. Durability against power loss still depends on the drive.

  • That a passing stage is a release

    A release has its own qualification, signed checksums and source binding.

Get started

Install 2.4.0 on Linux

# Download the archive and the signed checksum list, then verify
VERSION=2.4.0
ARCHIVE="zeno-fcis-$VERSION-x86_64-unknown-linux-gnu"
BASE=https://github.com/TheDarkLightX/ZenoFCIS/releases/download/v$VERSION
curl -fLO "$BASE/$ARCHIVE.tar.gz"
curl -fLO "$BASE/DOWNLOADS.sha256"
sha256sum --ignore-missing --check DOWNLOADS.sha256
tar -xzf "$ARCHIVE.tar.gz"
"$ARCHIVE/zeno-fcis" describe

macOS, source builds and the library API are in the installation guide.

The factory, live

Watch the checkers work

Each act runs the 2.4.0 library, compiled to WebAssembly, in a background worker on this page. Nothing here is a recording: every result is computed when you press the button, and nothing is sent to a server.

Act 1 · Synthesis

The checker chooses the rule order

When a request breaks two rules at once, the treasury guard’s order of checks decides which reason it gives: model or price, trade cap or daily budget, slippage or reserve. Synthesis tries all eight orders against the contract and keeps the one that is right on all 6,144 inputs.

Runs in this browser.

Act 2 · Every input

Plant a bug and watch it surface

The inventory contract is compiled here from its rules, and all 864 inputs are decided with every applicable law checked. Plant a bug in the rules and the same check shows each decision it changes.

Bugs come from the contract review’s mutation catalog.

Act 3 · Program search

A smaller program with the same answers

Search proposes a smaller version of the withdrawal queue’s 16-node decision kernel, and the checker compares it with the original on every input. A proposal with two outputs swapped is refused, with the input that shows it.

Deterministic; no model service is called.

The example supplies a 7-node starting point and search also tries its own rewrites; the result is not claimed to be the smallest possible program. The command-line loop also runs contained and issues receipts; this page does neither.

Act 4 · Money math

Exact rounding, and overflow refused

The Spot pilot’s swap: the fee rounds up, the output rounds down, and the product of the reserves may never decrease. A result that does not fit is refused, never wrapped.

Whole units, up to 3,000,000,000.

Act 5 · Try to break it

You are the agent

Send the treasury guard any swap you like. Every request that would break a law is refused, with its reason.

Open the challenge

The finite acts are checked on every input of their declared domain, in your browser; money math checks the inputs you supply and one overflow probe. Verus proofs cover the library’s source; the WebAssembly compiler and your browser are trusted. Nothing here issues a receipt, and none of it shows that a specification says what you meant.

This page’s interface is LLM-generated and tested in browsers. The decisions, checks, synthesis and search inside it are the library’s own code.

Proposers, judges, and the shell

A proposer submits a command. A person, service or AI agent can propose one; the checked core decides what it means.

The judge is the library's V2 authority. It checks the original request against the declared schema, evaluates the complete decision policy and every applicable law, and issues a publication only when those checks permit a commit. Each example supplies a declarative contract rather than an LLM-generated decision or law callback.

The imperative shell stores checked decisions and handles delivery. On a server it is SQLite with an outbox. This page uses an ephemeral browser store that consumes genuine publications and checks replay; it queues entries without delivering them. Its storage behavior is tested, not formally verified.

Each example below is a template exactly as zeno-fcis new writes it, built for WebAssembly without its SQLite shell. Its contract, authority and decision and law evaluators are the same source that decides on a server. The library's functional core has machine-checked proofs against its stated specifications, within a stated scope and named trusted base; the policy must still express the intended rules, and deployment assumptions remain explicit. The page only supplies each request and its context, including any time, and shows what the authority returned. The first section has already run its README's demonstration in this browser; every other example's module is fetched when its section is opened, and after that the page makes no network requests.

Account lockout

Three consecutive failed logins lock the account for 900 seconds. A failed login is a committed failure: the login fails, but the attempt is recorded. Every request carries its time, and the decision never reads a clock. Only a request marked admin may unlock. Alerts are queued in the outbox. The complete decision is evaluated from the example's V2 contract. The rules, in the order they apply, are in the template's README (crates/zeno-fcis-cli/templates/account-lockout/README.md in the repository).

What is checked, and at what strength
  • The V2 authority evaluates the complete declared policy over the original inputs, constructs the decision and checks every applicable law. A committing result carries a genuine publication; a rejection publishes nothing. The panel reports the actual law diagnostics.
  • The library's functional core has machine-checked proofs against its formal specifications, within a stated scope and named trusted base. The declared policy still needs review against the intended rules. Input authentication, browser storage, display and external delivery remain host concerns.
  • site/tests/replay.mjs runs 20 independent decision examples and the README's demonstration through this compiled module, comparing decisions, reasons, state, outbox and applicable laws. Every commit is followed by an actual idempotent replay. These integration checks detect disagreements; they do not prove the browser or a deployment correct.
  • Earlier synthesis, model and solver evidence is retained in the template's README and the kernel-law oracle tests. The earlier adapters described there do not execute this page's V2 decisions.
  • The time and admin flag are supplied inputs. A deployment must establish the caller’s permissions and trusted time.

Order fulfillment

One order from checkout to delivery. Payment and shipping happen in other systems: the order sends them requests through the outbox, and their answers come back as commands. Each request is numbered, so a provider can recognize a retry, and a callback that arrives twice or late is rejected and changes nothing. A declined payment is a committed failure: the order returns to Placed and keeps its attempt count. Its complete state machine is declared in the V2 contract. The rules, in the order they apply, are in the template's README (crates/zeno-fcis-cli/templates/order-fulfillment/README.md).

Compliance gateway

Transfers screened for one customer by an expert system's rule base: rules.txt holds twelve prioritized rules over finite features, and every decision names the rule that fired. A blocked transfer is a committed failure that adds a strike; three strikes freeze the account until a reviewer reinstates it. Review tickets and block alerts are queued in the outbox. The V2 contract declares the complete decision and its laws from the rule base. The rules are in the template's README (crates/zeno-fcis-cli/templates/compliance-gateway/README.md).

Withdrawal queue

One vault with two withdrawal lanes. Each lane's owner requests a withdrawal, and a keeper's tick pays at most one lane by following a checked controller. The tick's context carries an alarm; an honored alarm pauses payouts, but every requested withdrawal is still paid: alarms delay, they never freeze. Raise the alarm here and keep ticking to watch it. The V2 contract declares deposits, requests and the controller's tick behavior as one complete decision. The rules are in the template's README (crates/zeno-fcis-cli/templates/withdrawal-queue/README.md).

Act 5 · Try to break the treasury guard

You are the agent. Propose a swap that exceeds a limit and see whether the core refuses it. Try different requests: the scoreboard counts what happened and every returned decision still has to satisfy its laws.

About this example

A treasury that an AI agent trades for. The agent proposes swaps; its proposal is only a command, and the decision here is the guard. Whatever the agent proposes, the treasury never commits more than its daily budget, never drops below its reserve, never swaps outside the slippage bound against the oracle price, and never has more than one swap outstanding. Accepted swaps leave through the outbox as requests shaped after ZenoDEX's SwapIntent, and the DEX's settlements and failures come back as commands. Here the agent is scripted: a fixed list of proposals, good and bad, shown below as a proposer; no model is called. The library evaluates the guard's complete V2 contract over the original supplied facts. The rules are in the template's README (crates/zeno-fcis-cli/templates/agent-treasury-guard/README.md).
